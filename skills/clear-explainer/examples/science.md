# Science example: diffusion without a goal

This is a public, synthetic classroom example. Use it to calibrate a mechanistic
explanation and repair an intuitive but misleading story.

**Example request:** "Why does a dissolved dye spread through still water?"

**Essence:** In a simple diffusion model, molecules move through thermal motion.
Random motion produces a net spread from a region with higher concentration to a
region with lower concentration.

Imagine a tiny dye-rich region next to a dye-poor region at the same temperature.
Dye molecules can cross their shared boundary in either direction. Initially,
more dye molecules are available on the rich side, so more cross from rich to poor
than from poor to rich on average. The concentration difference decreases.

At equilibrium, molecules still move and cross the boundary; the average opposing
flows balance. They have not stopped, and individual molecules do not know which
side needs more dye.

**Analogy:** Randomly wandering people in two connected rooms can spread from a
crowded room into a less crowded one.

**Limit:** Molecules do not make decisions like people. Real liquids also involve
interactions, and visible dye spreading may include bulk fluid motion such as
convection. This explanation isolates diffusion rather than claiming every dye
experiment is explained by diffusion alone.

**Optional teach-back prompt:** Explain how random motion can create a net flow,
and what keeps happening when concentrations become equal. Wait for the learner's
attempt before giving corrective feedback.
